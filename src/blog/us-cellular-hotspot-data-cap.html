US Cellular Hotspot Throttling & Tethering Policy (2026)

Published September 23, 2026 · Updated October 5, 2026

Short answer: on most US Cellular unlimited plans, data used on the phone itself is unlimited, but mobile hotspot has its own, smaller high-speed allowance. When that allowance is used up, hotspot does not stop. It keeps working at a much lower speed until your billing cycle resets. On prepaid plans, hotspot shares the plan's high-speed data, and once that is gone the whole line slows down. Since August 2025, UScellular's wireless business belongs to T-Mobile, so the exact rules depend on whether you are still on a legacy UScellular plan or have moved to a T-Mobile plan.

What the US Cellular hotspot limit is

UScellular's unlimited tiers have generally included a set amount of high-speed hotspot data, and the amount varies by plan. That allowance is separate from the data you use on the phone itself, so it is possible to have plenty of phone data left while hotspot is already slow. On prepaid plans, hotspot use generally draws from the plan's overall high-speed data instead of a separate bucket.

Plan names and allowances have changed over the years, and many people are now in the middle of the move to T-Mobile. The number shown in your own account is the one that applies to you.

What happens when your hotspot data runs out

  • Unlimited plans: hotspot keeps working, but at a reduced speed until the next billing cycle. Browsing on the phone itself is not affected.
  • Prepaid plans: once the high-speed data is used, the whole line (phone and hotspot) moves to a slower speed until the plan renews.
  • Plans without hotspot: the hotspot may not turn on at all, or connected devices may not get data. This is usually a plan setting rather than a fault, so ask support whether hotspot is included on your line.

A slowed hotspot is usually fine for email and messaging, but video calls, large downloads and system updates on a laptop will feel very slow. For background on why carriers slow hotspot traffic, see why is my hotspot slow?

US Cellular after the T-Mobile deal

T-Mobile announced on August 1, 2025 that it had closed its acquisition of UScellular's wireless operations. In the same announcement, T-Mobile said UScellular customers stay on their existing plans with no changes for now, and can choose to move to one of T-Mobile's plans as networks and systems are integrated. UScellular business accounts are being moved to T-Mobile for Business, and the uscellular.com website now redirects to t-mobile.com.

What this means for your hotspot:

  • Still on a legacy UScellular plan: your plan's original hotspot allowance and rules apply until you change plans.
  • Moved to a T-Mobile plan: T-Mobile's hotspot allowances apply. See our T-Mobile hotspot guide for how they work and how to check them in the T-Life app.
  • Not sure which one you have: sign in to your account or ask support, since plan names changed during the transition.

(Checked October 2026 against T-Mobile's announcement and its UScellular pages. The transition is ongoing, so details can change.)

How to check your hotspot data

  1. Your carrier account Sign in to your account (or the T-Life app if you moved to T-Mobile) and open the usage details for your line. Look for a separate hotspot line item, not just total data.
  2. Your phone's data counter On Android, the hotspot or data usage screen shows how much connected devices used. On iPhone, go to Settings → Cellular and look for Personal Hotspot.
  3. Your billing date Note when your cycle resets, because that is when full hotspot speed comes back.
  4. Customer support Ask which hotspot allowance applies to your plan if your account does not show it clearly.

For a general checklist that works with any carrier, see how to check if your hotspot is really unlimited.

Your options when hotspot runs low

  1. Ask about a hotspot add-on or a bigger plan. If you tether every month, a plan with a larger hotspot allowance (including a T-Mobile plan, if you are eligible to move) may cost less than living with slow speeds.
  2. Wait for the reset. Use Wi-Fi for updates, backups and large downloads until your billing cycle starts again.
  3. Cut background data on the other device. Pause cloud sync, automatic updates and video autoplay on the laptop or tablet while it is connected to your phone.
  4. Confirm the terms first. Ask support how hotspot and tethering work on your plan before you change plans or rely on it for work.

Sharing your phone's connection with another device

There are several ways to share an Android phone's mobile data with a laptop, tablet or another phone: the built-in Wi-Fi hotspot, a USB cable, Bluetooth, or an app that shares the connection as a proxy over Wi-Fi. Each one has trade-offs in speed, battery use and setup. We compare them in 5 ways to share your phone's internet with another device.

Tether Link Utility is an Android app for the proxy approach: it shares your phone's mobile data with your other devices through a local SOCKS5 proxy, set up with a QR code. Whatever method you use, data used by the connected device still comes from your mobile line and counts toward your plan, so check your plan's tethering terms first.
