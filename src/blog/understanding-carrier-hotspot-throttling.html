Why Is My Hotspot Slow? Carrier Hotspot Limits Explained

Published September 16, 2026 · Updated September 25, 2026

If your laptop is slow on your phone's hotspot while the phone itself feels fast, there is usually a simple explanation. Many mobile plans treat hotspot (tethering) data differently from the data you use on the phone, and a few everyday factors can slow a hotspot down as well. This guide covers the common causes and how to check which one applies to you.

Hotspot data is often a separate allowance

Many plans sold as "unlimited" are unlimited for data used on the phone itself. Data shared with a laptop, tablet or another phone is often counted against a separate, smaller hotspot allowance. After that allowance is used, hotspot speeds are reduced for the rest of the billing period, while on-device data continues normally. Some plans instead count hotspot use against the same daily or monthly allowance as the phone, and some lines have no hotspot entitlement at all.

How carriers measure tethered data

Carriers generally can tell data used by the phone apart from data shared through its hotspot, and plan terms usually describe how each is counted. The practical point is simple: data used by connected devices counts toward your plan under your carrier's tethering terms, whichever way you connect them. The place to find out how your plan counts it is your plan details and your carrier's app.

Other reasons a hotspot can be slow

  • Network management. Some plans say hotspot or lower-priority data may be slowed first when a cell site is busy. This looks like speeds that jump around and recover at quieter times.
  • The Wi-Fi link between phone and laptop. Distance, walls, a crowded 2.4 GHz band or power-saving settings on the phone can limit the local connection.
  • Background use on the laptop. System updates, cloud backups and sync clients can use a lot of data without you noticing.
  • Signal at your location. Weak coverage affects the phone and hotspot alike, so compare the two at the same moment.

How to check which one it is

  1. Run a speed test on the phone and on the laptop at the same time and place. A large, steady gap points to a hotspot-specific limit.
  2. Open your carrier's app and look for a separate hotspot line in your plan's usage.
  3. Check your phone's own hotspot counter to see how much you have shared this cycle.

Official options if you need more hotspot data

  1. Buy a hotspot add-on or extra data package if your carrier sells one for your plan.
  2. Move to a plan with a larger hotspot allowance if you tether regularly.
  3. Wait for your allowance to reset on your billing date, or daily on plans with daily quotas.
  4. Use Wi-Fi for large downloads and pause laptop backups while tethering.
