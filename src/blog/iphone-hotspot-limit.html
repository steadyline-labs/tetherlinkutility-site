iPhone Hotspot Limits: How Many Devices and How Data Counts

Published September 23, 2026 · Updated October 31, 2026

Apple does not give a fixed number for how many devices can join an iPhone's Personal Hotspot at the same time: it depends on your carrier and your iPhone model. Your carrier is the one that can tell you the exact number for your line. Below you will find the three ways to connect, a checklist by symptom for when a new device won't join, and how your plan counts the data those devices use.

How many devices can connect

Apple's support page says it in one line: the number of devices that can join your Personal Hotspot at one time depends on your carrier and iPhone model, and your carrier has the details. The "5 devices" figure you often see online is not an Apple limit for today's iPhones. It dates back to 2011, when Personal Hotspot first came to the iPhone 4 and the number of connections was set per carrier.

The device count and your data allowance are two different things. However many devices you connect, the data they use all comes out of the same mobile plan. More on that in the data section below.

Three ways to connect a device

  • Wi-Fi: on the other device, pick your iPhone's name from the Wi-Fi list and enter the hotspot password. This is the most common way.
  • Bluetooth: on the other device, choose your iPhone in its Bluetooth settings. If a pairing code appears, make sure it is the same on both screens.
  • USB cable: plug the iPhone into a computer with a cable. On Windows, the Apple Devices app or iTunes needs to be up to date.

New device won't connect: checklist by symptom

  1. The other device doesn't see the iPhone at all. On the iPhone, open Settings > Personal Hotspot and check that Allow Others to Join is on. Stay on that screen until the other device connects.
  2. It sees the iPhone but won't connect, or drops right away. On the same screen, turn on Maximize Compatibility and connect again. Apple suggests this for newer iPhone models. While it is on, speed and Wi-Fi security for connected devices may be a little lower.
  3. It says the password is wrong. Check the password on the iPhone in Settings > Personal Hotspot. It must be at least 8 characters and use only English letters, numbers and common punctuation, so leave out accented or special letters.
  4. A few devices are connected and a new one won't join. You may have reached your limit. Disconnect a device you are not using: turn off its Wi-Fi or switch it to another network. Then try the new device again. Ask your carrier for the exact number.
  5. The Personal Hotspot menu is missing or won't turn on. Hotspot may not be enabled on your line. Check that Cellular Data is on, then ask your carrier whether your plan includes hotspot.
  6. Nothing above worked. Restart the iPhone and the other device, and update the iPhone. As a last step, go to Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Reset > Reset Network Settings. This also removes saved Wi-Fi passwords.
  7. Connected, but the internet is very slow. Your hotspot allowance may be used up. Check what is left in your carrier's app; our hotspot check guide shows where to look.

Connecting without a password with Family Sharing

Apple devices signed in to the same Apple Account, or in the same Family Sharing group, can join your iPhone's hotspot faster without typing the password. Both devices need Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on and should be close to each other. For family members, go to Settings > Personal Hotspot > Family Sharing on the iPhone and choose, for each person, whether they join automatically or need your approval each time.

How hotspot data counts toward your plan

iOS does not put a data limit of its own on Personal Hotspot. Your carrier decides how much hotspot data your plan includes and what happens once it is used, usually lower hotspot speed until the next billing period. Some plans count hotspot from the same pool as the rest of your data, some give it a separate allowance, and some do not include hotspot at all. If Personal Hotspot is missing from Settings or asks you to contact your carrier, the line usually has no hotspot included.

How to check your hotspot usage on iPhone

  1. Open Settings, then Cellular (Mobile Data in some regions).
  2. Tap System Services and look for Personal Hotspot, which shows data shared with other devices.
  3. This counter runs from the last time statistics were reset, not per billing cycle. Tap Reset Statistics at the bottom of the Cellular screen on your billing date if you want it to line up with your plan.

Your carrier's app remains the authoritative figure for what counts against your plan.

Official options when the data runs low

  • Buy a hotspot add-on or move to a plan with more hotspot data if your carrier offers one.
  • Wait for the reset on your billing date.
  • Use Wi-Fi for large downloads and pause automatic updates or backups on the other device while it is connected.

If the iPhone's limit or data isn't enough

If you have another Android phone, that phone can share its own mobile data with your devices too. Tether Link Utility is an Android app that does this through a local SOCKS5 proxy: the free version connects 1 device at a time, Pro connects 5. Data used by connected devices comes out of that Android phone's plan, and your carrier's tethering rules still apply, so check your plan first.

An iPhone or iPad can also connect to such an Android phone as a client, by installing Tether Link Utility from the App Store and scanning the QR code. No proxy settings needed. See the iPhone steps.
